What to remember while shooting. One stop = double (+1) or half (−1) the light.

## My films

| Film | Box | Rate at | Lab |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HP5+ | 400 | 400–1600, 3200 at a pinch | Push above 400 |
| XP2 Super | 400 | 50–800 | Normal C-41, never push |
| Portra 400 | 400 | 100–400 | Normal, never push |
| Portra 800 | 800 | 400–800 | Normal, never push |
| Ultramax 400 | 400 | 200–400 | Normal, never push |
| Metropolis XR | 100–400 | 100–400 | Normal C-41 at any EI in range |

- **Push** (B&W only): the lab develops longer to make up for underexposure. Every stop above box speed is one stop of push.
- **Colour negative** takes too much light far better than too little. Rate it at box speed or lower, never higher.
- **XP2** absorbs the whole 50–800 range with normal development. Past 800, use HP5.
- **LomoChrome Metropolis** (2021 formula): muted colours, punchy contrast, visible grain. XR = extended range: any EI from 100 to 400, even mixed on one roll, same development. Rated at 400 it tends to show less of its green cast.

## Pushing

| HP5 rated at | Tell the lab |
| --- | --- |
| 400 | Normal |
| 800 | +1 |
| 1600 | +2 |
| 3200 | +3 (grainy, contrasty) |

1. Decide before loading. One rating per roll: the lab develops the whole roll one way.
2. Set the ISO dial to the rating and log it as the EI when loading.
3. Mark the canister (`HP5 @1600`).
4. Tell the lab when you drop it off.

## Exposure

- **Colour negative: when in doubt, overexpose.** Shadows that got too little light can't be rescued.
- **B&W: expose for the shadows.** Don't let a bright sky make the meter underexpose everything else.
- **Backlight, snow, bright sky behind the subject: +1 to +2.** The meter averages to mid-grey and gets fooled by bright scenes.
- **Overexpose a whole roll:** rate it lower (Portra 400 at 200). No lab instructions needed for colour.
- **Overexpose one shot:** compensation dial +1, then back to 0.
- **Sunny 16** (needs a manual shutter speed): bright sun f/16 at 1/ISO. Light overcast f/11, heavy overcast f/8, open shade f/5.6, same shutter speed.

## Olympus OM-10

- **Aperture-priority automatic.** You pick the aperture, the camera picks the shutter speed.
- **Changing the aperture doesn't change the exposure**, only depth of field. The camera compensates with the shutter.
- **Exposure compensation is on the ISO dial.** It's the only way to give more or less light in auto.
- **Reset compensation to 0 after the shot.** The classic mistake: twenty frames at +1.
- **Manual shutter speeds** need the separate Olympus manual adapter.
- **Flash sync is 1/60 or slower.** Mode dial on Manual Adapter with no adapter fitted gives a fixed flash speed (see Flash).

## Flash: Vitacon Thyristor 828S

Auto-thyristor flash: a sensor on the front cuts the flash once the subject has had enough light. It doesn't talk to the camera.

**With the OM-10, every time:**

1. **Mode dial to Manual Adapter (no adapter fitted).** The shutter then fires at a fixed flash speed. In Auto the camera may pick faster than 1/60 and you get a black band across the frame.
2. **Flash ISO slider to the roll's EI.** The scale stops at 800: for HP5 @1600, set 800 and close the lens one stop more than the dial says.
3. **Pick an auto mode** with the switch on the front (the coloured dots), then set the lens to the aperture above the matching coloured tab on the dial.
4. **Stay inside the distance band** marked by the same colour under the scale. Orange reaches further, green is for close subjects.
5. **CHECK lights up after the shot** = the flash gave enough light. No CHECK: get closer or switch to the farther-reaching mode.
6. **Mode dial back to Auto** when you're done.

- **WIDE / NORMAL** slider on the dial: NORMAL for 35–50 mm lenses. WIDE only if a wide-angle panel is fitted on the flash head.
- **Bouncing:** tilt the head up (90 = straight up) towards a white ceiling. The sensor still faces the subject, but the light travels further: stay in the near half of the band and check the CHECK lamp.
- **Manual (no sensor):** aperture = guide number ÷ distance in metres. Reading the dial, the guide number is about 40 at ISO 400 (20 at ISO 100). Treat it as a starting point: old flashes often give less than rated.

*To check once, without film:* open the back, set Manual Adapter, point the flash at a wall and fire. You should see the whole frame lit through the shutter. Then measure the flash foot's voltage if you have a multimeter (under 6 V is safe on any camera).

## Harman Reusable Camera

Reloadable plastic point-and-shoot. Nothing to set: 31 mm lens at a fixed f/10, fixed 1/120 s, focus-free from 1 m.

- **Load ISO 400.** The exposure is fixed (about EV 13.5), so the film's latitude does all the work. 200 works in good light; anything slower underexposes outside full sun.
- **What the fixed exposure gives at ISO 400:** bright sun ≈ 3 stops over, overcast or open shade ≈ right, indoors far too little. Colour negative and XP2 shrug off the sun; for anything darker than open shade, use the flash.
- **Log the EI as the film's box speed** (Metropolis: 400). There is no ISO dial.
- **Flash:** push the switch, wait for it to charge (up to ~15 s, AAA battery). At f/10 it doesn't carry far: keep the subject within 1–3 m. Also useful as fill against backlight.
- **Viewfinder shows only ~70% of the frame**: the negative holds more around the edges than you framed. Nothing closer than 1 m; portraits from about 2 m.
- **Rewind: keep the rewind button pressed the whole time** and turn the crank gently. It's plastic and can snap.
